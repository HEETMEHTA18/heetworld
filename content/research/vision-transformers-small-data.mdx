# Vision transformers on small datasets

Transformers dominate language. In vision they win at scale — and struggle in the regime most practitioners actually live in: a few thousand images.

## The core tension

A ViT has no built-in prior about locality. On ImageNet-scale data, that freedom is a feature. On a small dataset it's a liability: the model overfits positional patterns instead of learning features.

## What the literature converges on

Reading across the key papers, three fixes recur:

1. **Distillation from a CNN teacher.** Give the ViT a strong inductive-bias teacher and let it learn spatial priors indirectly. The distilled variants beat plain ViTs at equal data with a fraction of the compute.
2. **Stronger augmentation.** Small-data training is a data problem; mixup, cutmix, and aggressive crop schedules matter more than any architectural tweak.
3. **Hybrid stems.** Starting with a convolutional patch stem reduces the "token poverty" problem — the model learns useful features before it ever sees the transformer blocks.

## My takeaway for my own CV work

For Retail Shelf Intelligence I defaulted to a YOLO-family CNN, not because transformers are worse, but because the *data regime* justified the prior. The lesson generalises: **match the architecture to the data budget, not to the benchmark leaderboard.**
